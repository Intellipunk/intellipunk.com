# Intellipunk Glossary

This glossary defines canonical Intellipunk terms. If a term is used differently elsewhere, update the glossary or fix the usage.

## Core concepts

**Intellipunk**  
A movement and design practice focused on preventing centralized intelligence (compute, models, data, and energy) from becoming an unmodifiable operating layer for society. Builds autonomy via local/edge compute, open models, verifiable execution, and energy realism.

**Centralized intelligence**  
Intelligence production controlled by a small set of entities through scale advantages in compute, energy contracts, data access, and proprietary models.

**AI empire**  
A durable concentration of intelligence capacity (models + compute + distribution) that creates dependency for everyone else.

**Agency (human agency)**  
The practical ability of people and communities to understand, modify, and exit the systems shaping their decisions and lives.

**Local autonomy**  
The capability to run useful intelligence without continuous permission, connectivity, or rent extraction from a central provider.

**Edge**  
Compute deployed close to users or communities (homes, buildings, micro-sites, local co-ops), not just hyperscale datacenters.

**Open models**  
Models whose weights and usage rights allow inspection, self-hosting, and modification (subject to licenses). “Open” is not the same as “free” or “safe.”

**Closed models**  
Models whose weights and internals are inaccessible; usage is gated by API policy, pricing, and compliance terms.

**Verifiable compute**  
Mechanisms that prove *what* code/model ran and *on what inputs* (within a defined trust model), enabling accountability and decentralized coordination.

**Energy realism**  
Treating energy as the binding constraint: watts, heat, intermittency, fuel risk, grid politics, and embodied costs. Rejects “infinite compute” fantasies.

**Compute as commons**  
Compute capacity governed like shared infrastructure: transparent rules, accountable operators, clear exit paths, and anti-monopoly defaults.

## Antagonisms (threat categories)

**Computational feudalism**  
A social arrangement where most people and orgs rent cognition from a few landlords (cloud/model owners) and become dependency-bound tenants.

**Extractive cloud**  
Cloud arrangements that centralize value by capturing data, lock-in, pricing power, and policy control while pushing risk/cost outward.

**Closed governance**  
Policy and model changes that affect everyone, decided privately with limited recourse, auditing, or interoperability.

**Grid capture**  
When large compute interests shape energy markets, interconnection queues, and infrastructure planning to privilege hyperscale loads over local resilience.

**Inference tax**  
Recurring rent paid per request/token/work unit, enforced by closed distribution channels and lack of viable alternatives.

**API serfdom**  
Operational dependency on a remote API for core capabilities, where outages, policy changes, or pricing shifts can disable products or communities.

## Infrastructure & primitives

**Guild node**  
A community-operated compute+energy node that contributes capacity to a shared network under explicit governance. Not “server in a closet”; it is a social contract + hardware.

**Circuit-Sun**  
Symbolic shorthand for the Intellipunk synthesis: computation constrained by physical energy, designed for local resilience.

**Micro-datacenter**  
Small-scale compute site (edge or community) designed for transparency, maintainability, and controllable blast radius.

**Self-hosting**  
Running models/services on hardware you control, with the ability to modify, audit, and exit.

**Federation**  
A network of autonomous nodes that cooperate via protocols rather than central ownership.

**Interoperability**  
The ability to swap components (models, runtimes, providers, wallets, attestations) without rebuilding the world.

**Data minimization**  
Default stance: collect less, retain less, centralize less. Design systems that do not require bulk data capture.

## Governance & economics

**Exit cost**  
The practical friction (technical, social, financial, legal) required to leave a platform or provider. Intellipunk seeks low exit cost.

**Anti-lock-in design**  
Architectural choices that preserve replaceability: standard formats, portable identities, clear state export, pluggable compute backends.

**Commons governance**  
Rules for shared infrastructure: who can join, how disputes resolve, how resources are allocated, how operators are audited.

**Rent extraction**  
Value capture that persists without proportional ongoing value creation, enforced by control points (distribution, licensing, energy contracts, IP).

**Proof-of-service**  
A verifiable record that a service was delivered (compute performed, job completed, model executed) suitable for accountability and payment.

## Operational/engineering terms

**Attestation**  
Cryptographic or hardware-backed evidence about the environment where code ran (e.g., enclave proofs). Useful but not magic.

**Reproducibility**  
The ability to obtain consistent outputs given defined versions of models, code, and inputs (within stochastic limits).

**Blast radius**  
How far failure propagates. Intellipunk prefers designs with small, local blast radii.

**Observability**  
Metrics/logs/traces sufficient to understand what is happening, without centralizing sensitive content by default.

## Style and usage rules

**“Intellipunk”**  
Capitalized, single word.

**Preferred terms**  
“centralized intelligence”, “compute monopoly”, “grid capture”, “verifiable compute”, “local autonomy”.

**Avoid / non-canonical**  
“decentralize everything”, “utopian”, “post-scarcity compute”, “AI will save us”.

**Working theory**  
Label speculative claims explicitly as “working theory” and specify the mechanism you’re assuming.

## Related movements (contrast terms)

**Cyberpunk**  
Dystopian high-tech, low-life; megacorps and surveillance dominate. Intellipunk inherits the anti-megacorp stance but focuses on intelligence concentration + energy.

**Solarpunk**  
Optimistic sustainable futures with community and renewables. Intellipunk shares energy focus but is less aesthetic-utopian and more adversarial to compute concentration.

**Hopepunk**  
Narratives emphasizing resistance and hope. Intellipunk can include hope, but requires concrete mechanisms and governance.
